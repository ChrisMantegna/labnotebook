1.1 Established Knowledge Areas by Committee Member

  • Alison- Aquatic Toxicology

    • Characteristics of PAHs, PCBs, and metals and their organism-impact profiles (not great wording for ‘what are they & what do they do in a biological sense’)

    • Oxidative stress mechanisms and manifestations

    • Biochemical responses including enzymatic activity and enzymatic effects

    • Detoxification pathways, including how that scales through biological scales, whole organism, and population or ecosystem-levels

    • Lab versus field-based study mechanisms, limitations, anticipated outcomes, and what each can or cannot predict re: organismal response.

  • Jose - Physiology

    • What physiological endpoints define a successful Mytilus phenotype, and how contaminants impact crucial pathways in growth, maintenance, and reproduction.

      • Generally, I should be able to write (and occasionally support with diagrams):

        • Foundational biological systems of Mytilus, and how those systems contribute to the successful growth and reproduction of each mussel.

          • Including mechanisms of action, interaction, and control or balance.

        • Detailed knowledge of the biological systems at play to maintain homeostasis, from cellular to organ system and back.

        • Detailed understanding of the endocrine control of growth and reproduction.

        • Answer, with specificity, how mussels are being affected by contaminant exposure; with a focus on PAHs, PCBs, metals, and endocrine disruptors.

  • Martha - Nearshore Ecology & Conservation

    • Primary driving question: How am I contextualizing my work within broader conservation efforts?

      • To answer this I should be able to answer:

      • Foundational/ classic nearshore ecology and conservation concepts and terminology (think Paine)

      • How are epigenetics studies adding to the conservation conversation, specifically how are we approaching physiological impact from this axis of investigation?

    • Large scale contextualization - what makes genetic work fundamental to conservation research?

      • What is the conservation puzzle I am solving, what puzzle pieces am I supplying, and what specifically can conservation efforts gain from these pieces?
