## Reflection & Next Steps

I made strong progress in both the teaching and degree progress goals,
and significantly less in the manuscript goal. Both of the first goals
require significantly more engagement and preparation with others to be
completed, and I prioritized ‘unblocking’ the tasks or content others
needed over the manuscript work.

### Goal 1: Manuscript Writing

The manuscript progress is less than I anticipated and I believe that is
because I spent too much time task switching between milestones. I have
reworked the narrative arc - shifting the context around the methylation
work from a ‘pilot’ to a planned extension, I’ve noted on the original
figures for both manuscripts what can be enhanced, removed, or
completely reworked, and I have a starting list for the supplement.
While none of these alone complete their respective milestones, the
progress is real. Moving forward, I have dedicated specific working
blocks to each milestone, added them to my working calendar across
November, and dropped a 2-week check-in reminder to myself to assess
progress more regularly.

### Goal 2: Teaching

Since I am teaching the 302 content for the first time, I spent much
more time understanding the material and developing lectures to ensure I
am connecting the content to the larger learning objectives. After a
month of engagement with the content and the class and my weekly
meetings with Lubna, I feel solid in my understanding and delivery.
Pivoting to November, I will not need to spend as much time on content
or lecture building, so this goal will be adapted to reflect that
change.

### Goal 3: Degree Milestone Progress

This goal is perhaps the most ‘logistically’ defined goal of the group.
Because there are specific departmental processes and timelines and
faculty schedules are challenging, this is one I did not want to
procrastinate on if I plan on graduating before the next century! While
most milestones are completed, this goal will carry into November as a
sub-goal of preparing for my written exam.
